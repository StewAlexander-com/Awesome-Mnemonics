## Problem-Solving Methodologies

### 8D (Eight Disciplines)

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ✓ Documented  
**Confidence:** Strongly validated

**Origin:**
- **Creator:** Ford Motor Company
- **Year:** 1987
- **Source:** *Team Oriented Problem Solving Manual*
- **Evolution:** Evolved from Total Quality Management (TQM); widely adopted in automotive and aerospace industries

**Citations:**
- Ford Motor Company. (1987). *Team Oriented Problem Solving Manual*. Ford Motor Company.
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [Eight Disciplines Problem Solving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_disciplines_problem_solving)
- ASQ (American Society for Quality). (n.d.). [What is the 8D Methodology?](https://asq.org/quality-resources/eight-disciplines-8d)

**Evidence:**
- Used by major automotive manufacturers (Ford, GM, Toyota)
- ISO 9001:2015 references 8D principles
- Widely taught in quality management courses
- Extensive case studies in manufacturing and IT

---

### 5 Whys

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ⚠ Widely adapted  
**Confidence:** Strongly validated

**Origin:**
- **Creator:** Sakichi Toyoda (attributed)
- **System:** Toyota Production System (TPS)
- **Era:** 1930s-1950s (developed as part of TPS)
- **Philosophy:** Root cause analysis through iterative questioning

**Citations:**
- Ohno, T. (1988). *Toyota Production System: Beyond Large-Scale Production*. Productivity Press.
- Liker, J. K. (2004). *The Toyota Way: 14 Management Principles from the World's Greatest Manufacturer*. McGraw-Hill.
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [5 Whys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_whys)

**Evidence:**
- Core technique in Lean Manufacturing and Six Sigma
- Taught in business schools and engineering programs
- Used by major corporations (Toyota, GE, Amazon)
- Adapted for software development (Agile, DevOps)

**Note:** While attributed to Toyota, the technique has been widely adapted across industries. The "5" is a guideline, not a strict rule—continue asking "why" until reaching a systemic root cause.

### Ishikawa (Fishbone) Diagram

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ✓ Documented  
**Confidence:** Strongly validated

**Origin:**
- **Creator:** Kaoru Ishikawa
- **Institution:** University of Tokyo
- **Era:** 1960s
- **Purpose:** Cause-and-effect analysis for quality control

**Citations:**
- Ishikawa, K. (1968). *Guide to Quality Control*. Asian Productivity Organization.
- Ishikawa, K. (1985). *What is Total Quality Control? The Japanese Way*. Prentice Hall.
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [Ishikawa Diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishikawa_diagram)

**Evidence:**
- Central to Japanese quality control movement
- Part of Seven Basic Tools of Quality (Ishikawa's contribution)
- Widely used in manufacturing, healthcare, software development
- ISO 9001 quality management systems reference cause-and-effect analysis

**Common Categories (6 M's):**
- Machine (equipment, technology)
- Method (process, procedure)
- Material (inputs, resources)
- Manpower (people, skills)
- Measurement (metrics, data)
- Milieu/Environment (context, conditions)

### A3 Problem Solving

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ✓ Documented  
**Confidence:** Strongly validated

**Origin:**
- **Creator:** Toyota Production System (TPS)
- **Format:** Single A3-size paper (11.7" × 16.5" / 297mm × 420mm)
- **Philosophy:** Plan-Do-Check-Act (PDCA) on one page for clarity and alignment
- **Era:** 1960s-1970s (developed as part of TPS)

**Citations:**
- Shook, J. (2008). *Managing to Learn: Using the A3 Management Process to Solve Problems, Gain Agreement, Mentor, and Lead*. Lean Enterprise Institute.
- Sobek, D. K., & Smalley, A. (2008). *Understanding A3 Thinking: A Critical Component of Toyota's PDCA Management System*. CRC Press.
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [A3 Problem Solving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_problem_solving)

**Evidence:**
- Core methodology in Lean Manufacturing
- Used by Toyota, Honda, and other Japanese manufacturers
- Adopted by healthcare (Virginia Mason Medical Center), software (Agile), and consulting
- Taught in Lean/Six Sigma certification programs

**Structure:**
1. Background & problem statement
2. Current state / gap analysis
3. Goal / target state
4. Root cause analysis (5 Whys or Ishikawa)
5. Countermeasures
6. Implementation plan & follow-up

## Strategic & Analysis Frameworks

### SWOT Analysis

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ✓ Documented  
**Confidence:** Industry standard

**Origin:**
- **Creator:** Albert Humphrey (attributed)
- **Institution:** Stanford Research Institute (SRI International)
- **Era:** 1960s-1970s
- **Context:** Developed during Fortune 500 strategic planning research

**Citations:**
- Humphrey, A. (2005). *SWOT Analysis for Management Consulting*. SRI International.
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [SWOT Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWOT_analysis)
- Ninety. (n.d.). [The SWOT Analysis and Strategic Planning Framework](https://www.ninety.io/hubfs/Founders%20Framework%20-%20The%20SWOT%20Analysis%20and%20Strategic%20Planning%20Framework.pdf)

**Evidence:**
- Taught in business schools worldwide
- Used by Fortune 500 companies for strategic planning
- Part of MBA curricula (Harvard, Stanford, Wharton)
- Standard tool in management consulting (McKinsey, BCG, Bain)

**Structure:**
- **S**trengths: Internal positive factors
- **W**eaknesses: Internal negative factors
- **O**pportunities: External positive factors
- **T**hreats: External negative factors

### PESTEL Analysis

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ✓ Documented  
**Confidence:** Industry standard

**Origin:**
- **Creator:** Francis Aguilar
- **Institution:** Harvard Business School
- **Year:** 1967
- **Source:** *Scanning the Business Environment*
- **Evolution:** Originally PEST (4 factors), extended to PESTLE (added Legal, Environmental)

**Citations:**
- Aguilar, F. (1967). *Scanning the Business Environment*. Macmillan.
- Paul, B. (2021). [Background & Development of PESTEL Analysis](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/background-development-pestel-analysis-biplab-paul-8hj0c)
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [PEST Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEST_analysis)

**Evidence:**
- Taught in strategic management courses
- Used in MBA programs (Harvard, INSEAD, London Business School)
- Standard tool in strategic planning and market analysis
- Extended variants: PESTLE, STEEPLE, PESTELI

**Structure:**
- **P**olitical: Government policies, regulations, political stability
- **E**conomic: Economic growth, inflation, exchange rates
- **S**ociocultural: Demographics, culture, social trends
- **T**echnological: Innovation, R&D, automation
- **E**nvironmental: Climate, sustainability, resource availability
- **L**egal: Laws, regulations, compliance requirements

### RACI (Responsibility Assignment Matrix)

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ✓ Documented  
**Confidence:** Industry standard

**Origin:**
- **Era:** 1950s-1970s
- **Context:** Emerged from project management and organizational theory
- **Note:** No single inventor; evolved from management practices

**Citations:**
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [Responsibility Assignment Matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Responsibility_assignment_matrix)
- PMI (Project Management Institute). (2017). *A Guide to the Project Management Body of Knowledge (PMBOK Guide)*. 6th Edition.
- OGC (Office of Government Commerce). (2009). *Managing Successful Projects with PRINCE2*.

**Evidence:**
- Standard tool in project management (PMI PMBOK, PRINCE2)
- Used in organizational design and change management
- Taught in project management certification programs
- Widely adopted in IT, construction, and consulting

**Structure:**
- **R**esponsible: Does the work
- **A**ccountable: Final approval (only one per task)
- **C**onsulted: Provides input
- **I**nformed: Kept updated

**Variants:** RACI-VS (Verifies, Signs off), RAPID (Recommend, Agree, Perform, Input, Decide)

### SET (Systems Engineering Triangle)

**Rating:** ⭐⭐⭐⭐  
**Status:** ✓ Documented  
**Confidence:** Industry standard

**Origin:**
- **Source:** SEBoK (Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge)
- **Concept:** "Good, Fast, Cheap: Pick Two" — project management trade-off triangle
- **Context:** Systems engineering and project management

**Citations:**
- SEBoK. (n.d.). [Guide to the Systems Engineering Body of Knowledge](https://www.sebokwiki.org/wiki/Guide_to_the_Systems_Engineering_Body_of_Knowledge_(SEBoK))
- Wikipedia. (n.d.). [Project Management Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_management_triangle)

**Evidence:**
- Core concept in systems engineering education
- Taught in engineering and project management programs
- Used in software development (Agile, Scrum)
- Standard tool for setting stakeholder expectations

**Principle:**
- **Good + Fast ≠ Cheap** (Quality + Speed = Higher Cost)
- **Fast + Cheap ≠ Good** (Speed + Low Cost = Lower Quality)
- **Good + Cheap ≠ Fast** (Quality + Low Cost = Slower Delivery)
